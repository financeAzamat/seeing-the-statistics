Seeing the statistics

Interactive explanations of the ideas in the UDJ course. Every one runs on real published data — no invented numbers, no scripted animations — and every figure quoted in the text is checked against the data by a script that ships beside it.

  • 01 Where your judgment goes wrong Six questions about real data, answered by you before the measurement is revealed — and a scorecard that shows whether your misses fall on the same side, which is what makes a bias correctable. ready
  • 03 The average is not typical Why more than three in four real orders come in below the "average" order, and why on two-humped data no single number can describe a typical case at all. ready
  • 04 Correlation and the range you looked at Why the same relationship between size and price scores +0.92 or +0.09 depending only on which stones were in the sample — and why splitting two clusters apart destroys a correlation of +0.90. ready
  • 05·1 The Central Limit Theorem Why the average of 30 things is dependable when one thing is not — and why "n ≈ 30" is a rule about tails, not a constant. ready
  • 05·2 Confidence intervals What "95% confident" promises, why it describes the method rather than your answer, and where it quietly delivers 86% instead. ready
  • 06 p-values and hypothesis tests Where the 5% false-alarm rate comes from, why testing twenty things manufactures a winner, and why "not significant" usually means the test was never able to see it. ready
  • 07 Regression and R² Drag the line and fail to beat least squares. Then find that the two datasets with the highest R² are the two you should trust least. ready
  • 07·2 Overfitting and adjusted R² Add columns of pure noise and watch R² climb. Then watch the held-back score collapse through zero. ready
  • 08 Regression diagnostics Four questions to ask a line, each scored pass or fail — then watch a transform repair three of them at once, and meet the one it cannot. ready
  • 09 Accuracy and the confusion matrix 97% accurate and completely useless. Drag the decision line and watch precision and recall trade against each other. ready
  • + Simpson's paradox The one number that does not merely mislead — it points the wrong way. Real diamonds, and every subgroup disagrees with the total. ready
Data: UCI Online Retail (real UK e-commerce invoices), Old Faithful eruption waits and durations, and ggplot2 diamond carats, prices and quality grades. Built by the scripts in data/.
Checks: eleven verification scripts. verify_stats_js.py runs the shipped p-value, inverse-normal and least-squares code under node against scipy and numpy; verify_render.py executes all eight renderers headlessly against a stubbed canvas; verify_extras.py re-derives every diamond average from the source CSV cell by cell; the rest match every figure in the prose against the measurement that produced it.
Thirteen errors have been caught by these scripts rather than by review — including two invented comparisons, a measurement seeded with Python's per-process hash(), a diagnostic computed on the full source while the page plotted a sample, a globalAlpha above 1 that the spec silently ignores, and four checks that quietly stopped matching after a rewording.
