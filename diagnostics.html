UDJ · Module 8 — Regression Diagnostics

Four questions to ask a line

Because a fit can look excellent and still be wrong.

Module 7 ended on an uncomfortable note: the two datasets with the highest R² were the two least worth trusting. This page is how you would have caught that. Four checks, four panels, and a pass or fail on each — and then the repair.

The four questions

  1. Is the shape right? The errors should average out to zero everywhere along x. If they are positive at the ends and negative in the middle, the truth is a curve and you fitted a line. bend < 0.5
  2. Is the spread even? A line that is accurate for small values and wild for large ones has one number for its accuracy and it is a lie in both directions. fan < 2
  3. Is each error independent of the last? If knowing one error helps you guess the next, the data carries structure the model has not been told about. |lag-1| < 0.15
  4. Are the errors bell-shaped? Every p-value and interval from Modules 5 and 6 assumed they were. Heavy tails make those numbers optimistic. |skew| < 1 and kurtosis < 2

Watch a fit get repaired

  1. Start on diamonds, transform none. Three of the four panels fail. R² is 0.84 and the fit is unusable at the top end.
  2. Switch to log both. The fan collapses from 13.4 to 1.6, the tails come in from a kurtosis of 7.2 to 0.5, and R² rises from 0.84 to 0.93.
  3. Now try log y on its own — the half-measure. The fan improves, and the bend gets worse: 0.82 → 1.78. The wrong transform is not a smaller version of the right one.
  4. Switch to geyser. Try every transform. Nothing helps, because what it breaks is not fixable by rescaling.

Taking logs of both sides took the diamonds from a fit that failed three checks to one that nearly passes all four — and explained more. A repair that also improves the fit is not a trade-off, it is evidence that the original shape was simply wrong.

The one you cannot transform away

The geyser breaks independence. Consecutive errors correlate at −0.28 (Durbin-Watson 2.54, where 2 means no autocorrelation) — negative, which means a bigger-than-expected wait tends to be followed by a smaller one.

The reason is physical, and checkable: Old Faithful alternates. 67% of consecutive eruptions switch kind, short to long or long to short, against 50% for a coin flip. So the previous eruption tells you something the model never sees.

No amount of rescaling fixes a missing variable. The repair here is a different model — one that includes the previous eruption — not a different unit for this one.

The check you often cannot run

Independence only means something if the rows have a real order. The geyser's do: 272 eruptions in the sequence they happened. The diamonds are a cross-section — stones that exist simultaneously — and the retail points are a random sample, which destroys order by construction.

So for those two the page refuses to score that panel and says why, rather than printing a lag-1 figure that would only be measuring the order of a file. A diagnostic you cannot run is not a diagnostic you passed.

And one point can move everything

The panel also rings the single most influential point — the one with the largest Cook's distance, which combines being unusual in x with being badly predicted. Press drop it and watch the slope jump.

On the retail orders, removing one invoice out of 800 moves the slope by 5.3%. That is not a reason to delete it: the right response is to find out what it was. But it is a reason to know it is there before you quote the number to three decimals.

The same thing, in exam language

homoskedasticity
Constant error variance. The spread panel. Broken by the diamonds at 13.4×.
autocorrelation
Errors correlated with their own neighbours. Measured by Durbin-Watson ≈ 2(1 − r₁): near 2 is clean, below 2 positive, above 2 negative.
leverage
hᵢ = 1/n + (xᵢ−x̄)²/Σ(xⱼ−x̄)². How far out in x a point sits, regardless of whether it is fitted well.
Cook's distance
Leverage combined with a large residual — how much the fitted line would move if the point were dropped. Ringed on the scatter.
skewness / excess kurtosis
Third and fourth standardised moments. 0 and 0 for a normal; the retail residuals are +4.2 and +23.
specification error
The shape panel failing. Not a data problem — a model problem, and the only one of the four that R² partly reflects.

The order in the course's model-building steps is the order here: fit, check the F-test, prune, look for outliers, then run these residual diagnostics — and go back to the start if any of them fails.

Every figure on the panels is computed live from the points on screen by stats.js, using the same formulas as data/measure_diagnostics.py. verify_diagnostics.py runs the shipped JavaScript under node and checks it agrees with the Python to 1e-9 on all three datasets and all three transforms — so the pass/fail badges are reproducible outside the browser.
