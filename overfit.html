UDJ · Module 7 — Overfitting & Adjusted R²

R² can be bought

Add columns of pure noise and watch it rise.

R² has a property nobody mentions when quoting it: it can never go down when you add a predictor. Not "rarely" — never. So "we got R² up to 0.88" is not, on its own, a claim that anything improved.

The experiment

Take a real relationship. Add columns of random numbers — provably meaningless, generated on the spot, unrelated to anything. Refit. Repeat.

Three lines are tracked as the junk piles up:

  • R² on the data it was fitted to — climbs, and must.
  • Adjusted R² — the same number with a penalty for each predictor. It can fall, and does.
  • R² on data held back — the model has never seen it. This is the only line that answers "is the model any good?", and it collapses.

Why R² cannot fall

Least squares picks the coefficients that make the error as small as possible. A new column can always be given a coefficient of zero, which reproduces the old fit exactly. So the best fit with the extra column is never worse than the best fit without it — and with real numbers it is always very slightly better, because random noise always correlates a little with anything by chance.

R² = 1 − SSE/SST ← cannot rise as columns are added adj = 1 − (1−R²)·(n−1)/(n−k−1) ← pays k for the privilege

The adjustment divides by n − k − 1. Every predictor costs you a degree of freedom, so the penalty grows as k approaches n. That is why adjusted R² turns down while raw R² keeps climbing.

Try it yourself

  1. Watch the lines draw themselves — the page fits a fresh model at each step rather than reading a table.
  2. Push predictors up. The blue line climbs the whole way. It has to.
  3. Watch the red line. It peels away and then dives, often below zero — which means the model is worse than ignoring the data and guessing the average.
  4. Switch to geyser. With only 136 rows to train on, the collapse is brutal: the model is memorising rather than learning.

A negative held-back R² is the clearest signal in statistics: the model has learned the noise in the sample it saw and carries no information about anything else.

What to do about it

  • Quote adjusted R², not R², the moment there is more than one predictor. It is free and it is honest.
  • Hold data back. Nothing else answers the question. Fit on part, score on the rest, report that number.
  • Be suspicious of "we added features and R² improved." That sentence is true of random numbers.
  • Count your predictors against your rows. As k approaches n, a model can fit anything perfectly and predict nothing.

In exam language

R² monotonicity
Adding a regressor cannot increase SSE, because the zero coefficient is always available. Hence R² is non-decreasing in k.
adjusted R²
1 − (1−R²)(n−1)/(n−k−1). Penalises k; can decrease and can go negative.
degrees of freedom
n − k − 1. At k = n − 1 the fit is exact and the model has no residual freedom left — R² = 1, information = 0.
in-sample vs out-of-sample
The distinction the F-test and t-tests are protecting you from. The course's backward-elimination step exists to stop k growing without evidence.
the F-test
Asks whether the whole set of slopes is jointly zero. It is the formal version of the question this page asks by brute force.
