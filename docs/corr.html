UDJ · Module 4 — Correlation

The same relationship, six different answers

r does not measure a relationship. It measures a relationship and the window you looked through.

Bigger diamonds cost more, and that fact does not change. The correlation between size and price is +0.92 or +0.09 depending only on which stones you were allowed to see, and on nothing about the stones themselves.

What r is supposed to tell you

The correlation coefficient runs from −1 to +1. Near +1 means the two things rise together tightly; near 0 means knowing one tells you nothing about the other. It is quoted everywhere because it is a single tidy number with no units, which makes it look like a property of the pair of things being measured.

Correlation is a property of your sample rather than of the pair of things being measured.

Watch it collapse

Here is the identical relationship — every stone from the same table, the same prices — measured through progressively narrower windows on size:

Stones you can seeHow manyr
all sizes53,940+0.922
0.50–1.20 ct27,111+0.799
0.90–1.10 ct10,331+0.406
0.99–1.01 ct3,823+0.088

Between the first row and the last, the correlation loses 90% of its size. Not one price changed. Not one stone was re-cut. The only thing that changed is how wide a range of sizes the sample contained.

Why this happens

Correlation is a ratio. It compares how much y moves with x against how much y moves in total. Shrink the range of x and you shrink the part it can explain, while all the other reasons prices differ — cut, colour, clarity, luck — carry on unchanged. The signal gets smaller and the noise does not.

narrow the window on x the part y moves because of x shrinks · everything else stays → r falls

Among stones that all weigh almost exactly one carat, size genuinely has almost nothing left to say about price. r = +0.088 is the correct answer to a much narrower question, and it would be a terrible answer to "does size drive price?"

The same trap, running the other way

Old Faithful erupts in two habits: short bursts followed by a short wait, long bursts followed by a long wait. Across all 272 eruptions, the length of an eruption and the wait that follows correlate at +0.901, which reads as a tight, almost mechanical relationship.

Split it by habit and it disappears:

EruptionsHow manyr
all of them272+0.901
short, under 3 min97+0.290
long, 3 min or more175+0.373

That +0.901 is almost entirely the gap between the two groups rather than agreement inside them. Knowing which habit you are in tells you a lot. Knowing the exact length of the eruption, once you know the habit, tells you very little.

Both cases are the same mechanism. A wide spread in x inflates r; a narrow spread deflates it. Two separated clusters are the widest possible spread, which is why splitting them apart destroys the number.

And r only ever looks for a straight line

One more limit worth knowing. Size and price for diamonds rise together almost perfectly — but not in a straight line, because price grows faster than weight. Score the same data three ways:

What is being scoredScore
r — how straight-line the rise is+0.922
rank correlation — does it rise at all+0.963
r after taking logs of both+0.966

The relationship was always that tight. The +0.922 is r being docked marks for a curve, not evidence of a weaker relationship.

Where this appears in practice

Range restriction is not an exotic edge case. It is the normal condition of most business data, because most data is already filtered:

  • In hiring, you measure how well an aptitude test predicts performance using only the people you hired — who all scored well. The test looks useless because you removed the low scores.
  • In credit, default models are fitted on approved loans. Everyone obviously risky was declined, so the risk factors look weak.
  • In pricing, checking whether price affects demand using last quarter's prices, which barely moved, and price looks irrelevant.
  • Pooling two regions, two products or two seasons and a correlation appears that exists in neither of them — the mirror image, and the same arithmetic.

Before you trust an r, ask what range of x is in the sample and whether it is the range you care about. A low correlation on a narrow slice is not evidence of no relationship, and a high one across two clumps is not evidence of a relationship.

In exam language

The Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient is cov(x,y)/(σxσy). Restricting the range of x reduces σx and the covariance together, but leaves the residual variance of y unchanged, so the attenuation is systematic rather than sampling noise. Thorndike's correction estimates the unrestricted coefficient when the selection variance is known.

For a simple linear regression, R² = r², so everything Module 7 says about R² applies here squared. What r adds is that it is symmetric and unitless: it asserts no direction of causation and carries no slope. A slope of £1 per carat and £10,000 per carat can share an r.

Spearman's rho is Pearson's r on ranks and detects any monotone relationship, not only a linear one. The gap between +0.922 and +0.963 here is the cost of measuring a convex relationship with a linear statistic.

The geyser case is aggregation-induced correlation across a mixture: r computed on a pooled two-component mixture is dominated by the separation of the component means. This is the correlational form of the Module 5 warning about averaging across a mixture, and a cousin of Simpson's paradox — there the sign reverses, here only the magnitude is manufactured.

Two real datasets, no simulation. Every coefficient above is computed on the full population inside its window — all 53,940 stones, all 272 eruptions — and re-derived from the raw CSVs by verify_corr.py. The diamond scatter draws a seeded random sample of 1,200 stones so the plot stays legible; the panel states both counts, because a figure measured on the population and drawn from a sample is exactly the sort of mismatch that produces a wrong number.

A footnote on real dirt: the diamonds table contains 20 rows with a physically impossible zero dimension and 3 stones recorded as over 2 cm. Dropping those 23 rows of 53,940 moves the width-to-length correlation from +0.975 to +0.999. Twenty-three bad rows in fifty-four thousand were holding a coefficient down by that much.
