Course · UDJ — Uncertainty, Data & Judgment

Seeing the statistics

UDJ — decision-making under uncertainty. Eleven of the course's ideas, here as things you can push on — on real data.

  • 01 Where your judgment goes wrong Answer six questions. ready
  • 03 The average is not typical More than three in four real orders come in below the "average". And on two-humped data there is no typical case at all. ready
  • 04 Correlation and the range you looked at The same relationship between size and price scores +0.92 or +0.09. The only difference is which stones were in the sample. ready
  • 05·1 The Central Limit Theorem Why an average of 30 is dependable when one observation is not. And why "n ≈ 30" is a rule about tails, not a constant. ready
  • 05·2 Confidence intervals "95% confident" is a promise about the method, not about your answer. And sometimes it delivers 86%. ready
  • 06 p-values and hypothesis tests Test twenty things and a "winner" appears on its own. And "not significant" usually means the test could never have seen it. ready
  • 07 Regression and R² Drag the line and fail to beat least squares. Then find that the two datasets with the highest R² are the two you should trust least. ready
  • 07·2 Overfitting and adjusted R² Add columns of pure noise and watch R² climb. Then watch the held-back score collapse through zero. ready
  • 08 Regression diagnostics Four questions to ask a line, each scored pass or fail. A fit can look excellent and still be wrong. ready
  • 09 Accuracy and the confusion matrix 97% accurate and completely useless. Drag the decision line and watch precision and recall trade against each other. ready
  • + Simpson's paradox The one number that does not merely mislead — it points the wrong way. Real diamonds, and every subgroup disagrees with the total. ready
Coverage: modules 1 and 3–9. Module 2 has no page of its own — its material is worked through in module 3.
Data: UCI Online Retail (real UK e-commerce invoices), Old Faithful eruption waits and durations, and ggplot2 diamond carats, prices and quality grades. Built by the scripts in data/.
Checks: eighteen verification scripts plus a claims audit. verify_stats_js.py runs the shipped p-value, inverse-normal and least-squares code under node against scipy and numpy; verify_render.py executes all eleven renderers headlessly against a stubbed canvas; verify_extras.py re-derives every diamond average from the source CSV cell by cell; the rest match every figure in the prose against the measurement that produced it.
Every error listed here was caught by a script rather than by review — including two invented comparisons, a measurement seeded with Python's per-process hash(), a diagnostic computed on the full source while the page plotted a sample, a globalAlpha above 1 that the spec silently ignores, a peak finder that reported one cluster twice, a choice drawn off the end of its own scale, and several checks that quietly stopped matching after a rewording.
